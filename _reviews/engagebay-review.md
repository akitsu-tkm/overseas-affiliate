## The problem: your contact list lives in four different tools, and none of them agree on what happened last

Here's a familiar shape for a small B2B team: leads come in through a landing page tool, get emailed through a separate marketing platform, get worked by sales in a spreadsheet or a barebones CRM, and when they need help post-sale, that goes to a shared inbox nobody else can see the history of. Each piece does its one job fine. The trouble starts the moment someone needs the full picture — a salesperson trying to close a deal without knowing the prospect already emailed support twice with a complaint, or a marketer sending a "still interested?" nurture email to someone who signed a contract two days ago because the marketing tool has no idea sales closed them.

That's the specific gap this category of software is built to close: one system where a contact's marketing history, sales stage, and support tickets all live in the same record, instead of four systems that each know a quarter of the story. The tools below all chase that same goal, but they start from different price points and bundle the pieces differently — some genuinely fold marketing, sales, and support into one base price, others sell CRM as an add-on bolted onto an email tool, and one skips tiers entirely in favor of a single higher-priced all-inclusive plan. Picking the wrong starting point here is how a five-person team ends up paying for three separate subscriptions that each do a third of the job, or signs up for an all-in-one platform priced for a team five times their size.

I helped a friend's six-person consulting shop untangle exactly this a while back. They were running HubSpot's free CRM for contacts, a separate email tool for their monthly newsletter, and a shared Gmail inbox for client support questions — three logins, three places a contact's information could go stale, and a recurring problem where a client would email a question that support answered, then get a generic marketing email two days later that ignored the conversation entirely. Consolidating into one platform where the support ticket, the deal stage, and the email history sat on the same contact record didn't just save logins — it fixed a real client-facing embarrassment that had happened more than once. That's the workflow problem worth solving before comparing feature checklists line by line.

## Three things that actually matter when you're choosing

**1. Whether marketing, sales, and support are genuinely bundled, or whether one of them is an add-on.** This is the single biggest structural difference in this comparison. EngageBay, Zoho CRM, Freshsales, and Keap all build CRM and pipeline tracking into their core plans from the entry tier up. ActiveCampaign is the clear exception here: its cheapest Starter plan is email and automation only, and Pipelines — its CRM and deal-tracking layer — is sold as a separate add-on across every tier rather than included. HubSpot splits the difference in its own way: the free CRM is genuinely free and full-featured for basic contact and deal tracking, but marketing, sales, and service each live in their own "Hub" with separate paid tiers, so a team that wants all three at a meaningful feature depth is often pricing out three subscriptions, not one.

**2. What the free or entry tier actually locks you out of.** A free plan that's technically free but missing the one feature a team needs on day one isn't really free for that team. EngageBay's free plan supports up to 15 team members but caps out at 250 contacts. HubSpot's free CRM allows 1,000 contacts but only 2 users. Zoho CRM and Freshsales both cap their free tiers at 3 users. None of these numbers is objectively better — a 15-person team with a small list fits EngageBay's free tier well; a 2-person team building a large list fits HubSpot's free CRM better. The right move is checking which ceiling — user count or contact count — actually matches how the team is shaped, not just glancing at "free" and assuming it fits.

**3. How pricing scales as the team or contact list grows.** Per-seat pricing (EngageBay, Zoho CRM, Freshsales, ActiveCampaign) means the bill grows with headcount, which is predictable for a small team but can get expensive fast for a larger one. Keap breaks from that model with a single flat plan that includes two users and prices additional seats separately — a genuinely different structure that makes more sense for a business that wants one predictable contract rather than a per-seat calculation. Contact-based tiers (most of these tools also gate features by contact-list size, not just seats) add a second dimension worth checking before assuming a quoted starting price is the actual number a growing list will land on a year later.

## Which one fits your situation

**You're a small team (up to 15 people) that wants marketing, sales, and support genuinely combined without separate subscriptions for each:** EngageBay's free plan is a reasonable starting point if the contact list is still under 250, with a clear per-user upgrade path once it grows.

**You want the most widely recognized CRM brand and the broadest integration ecosystem, and 2 free users covers the team for now:** HubSpot's free CRM, with the Starter Customer Platform as the next step once marketing or service tools are needed alongside it.

**Price is the deciding factor and the free tier's contact cap matters more than its user cap:** Freshsales, whose free tier has no contact limit at all, or Zoho CRM if a longer runway of paid tiers to grow into matters more than the absolute lowest entry price.

**You're an established small business that wants one flat-priced contract covering CRM, automation, and payments, and would rather not manage feature-gated tiers:** Keap, understanding upfront that its entry price sits well above the rest of this comparison.

**Email marketing and automation depth is the actual priority, and CRM is a nice-to-have rather than a day-one requirement:** ActiveCampaign, with the understanding that Pipelines (its CRM layer) is a separate purchase, not something bundled into the base plan the way it is with EngageBay, Zoho CRM, or Freshsales.

## Where to start

The fastest way to tell these apart isn't reading a feature list — it's importing one real segment of an actual contact list (not a handful of test entries) into whichever free plan fits the team's shape, then walking one contact through the full loop: an inbound lead, a sales conversation, and a support question, all on the same record. That single test surfaces whether a tool's "all-in-one" claim holds up in practice, or whether marketing, sales, and support quietly stay three separate silos with a shared login screen.

For a small B2B team specifically weighing EngageBay against HubSpot's free CRM, the practical tiebreaker is usually which ceiling gets hit first: team size or contact count. A team of ten with a modest list fits EngageBay's 15-user free plan comfortably. A two-person team building toward a large list will bump into EngageBay's 250-contact cap before HubSpot's 1,000-contact one. Either way, both are free to start — the honest move is testing with real data before deciding, rather than picking based on brand recognition alone.

Start free with EngageBay's own free plan to see whether having CRM, email marketing, live chat, and helpdesk on one contact record actually removes the coordination gap a spreadsheet-and-separate-inbox setup leaves behind — no credit card required, and the free tier is generous enough on team size to bring the whole small team in from day one rather than testing solo.
