## The problem: the AI made a deck in ten seconds, and now you have to actually use it

Every tool in this category opens with the same demo: type a topic, watch an AI assemble a full slide deck in under a minute, and the pitch practically writes itself. That part genuinely works, across all five tools below. What the demo doesn't show is what happens next — reworking the slide that has the wrong emphasis, exporting to the format your client's email client can actually open, or hitting a monthly AI-credit ceiling halfway through revising a deck for a meeting that's already on the calendar.

I put together a partner-facing deck a while back under a tight deadline, bouncing between two of these tools mid-project because the first one ran out of AI credits before the outline was even finished being revised. That's the failure mode this category doesn't advertise: the generation step is fast and impressive everywhere, but the tools diverge hard on how much AI-assisted editing you actually get for the price, how painful it is to fix a slide the AI got wrong, and whether the export you need is even included.

None of the five tools below are interchangeable, despite looking similar in a two-minute demo video. Some are built for AI-first speed with real design-eye layout logic underneath (Beautiful.ai). Some are general-purpose design platforms that happen to do presentations well among many other formats (Canva). Some lean hard into a minimal, opinionated aesthetic (Pitch). One is closer to a document-and-deck hybrid that generates from a prompt or an outline (Gamma). Picking based on the demo alone is how people end up paying for AI credits they burn through in the first week.

## Three things that actually matter when you're choosing

**1. How AI credits are metered, and what happens when you run out.** This is the detail every pricing page buries below the fold. Every tool here uses some version of a credit system for AI generation and regeneration, and the credit allowances vary by a wide margin — from a few hundred a month on entry paid tiers to tens of thousands on the top tier of some tools. What matters in practice isn't the headline number, it's whether regenerating a single slide costs one credit or several, and whether you can keep manually editing a deck after credits run out (usually yes) or whether AI-assisted editing specifically locks up (varies by tool).

**2. How much manual control you get once the AI-generated draft is wrong.** Every one of these tools nails the first draft in a demo. The real test is slide four, where the AI put the wrong stat in bold or laid out a chart nobody asked for. Some tools (Beautiful.ai in particular) use a smart-layout engine that keeps re-flowing the design as you edit, which is fast but occasionally fights you if you want a very specific manual layout. Others (Canva, Pitch) give you a more traditional freeform editor once the AI step is done, trading some of that automatic polish for direct control.

**3. Export format and where the deck actually needs to live.** Not every tool exports cleanly to PowerPoint, and that matters more than it sounds like if the deck's final audience is a client or an executive who opens things in PowerPoint or Keynote rather than viewing a hosted web link. A couple of tools in this comparison also gate PDF or PPT export, or brand-free export, behind the paid tier entirely — worth checking before assuming a "free" deck is actually shareable the way you need it to be.

## The breakdown, tool by tool

### Decktopus

**What it's for:** people who present the deck themselves and want built-in rehearsal tooling, not just a deck-building tool. The Loop feature — practicing a pitch out loud against the deck — is the one thing here none of the four competitors offer.

**Pros**
- 750 AI credits/month on the entry Pro tier is a meaningfully larger monthly allowance than several competitors' entry tiers
- The Loop rehearsal feature is a genuine differentiator for anyone who actually delivers the presentation live, rather than just sending a deck as a document
- Cancel-anytime billing with no early-termination fee, and a 50% education discount for students and educators

**Cons**
- No published free tier and no stated trial length, so there's no low-risk way to test the editor before paying
- Business-tier pricing isn't public, which makes it hard to budget for a team upgrade without a sales conversation
- Smaller ecosystem and community than Canva or Gamma, so third-party templates and tutorials are harder to find

### Canva

**What it's for:** anyone who wants presentations to sit inside the same tool they already use for social graphics, one-pagers, and other design work, sharing one Brand Kit across everything rather than maintaining brand assets separately in a presentation-only tool.

**Pros**
- The same enormous template and stock library used across every other Canva format also covers presentations, so there's rarely a starting-from-scratch problem
- Brand Kit and existing brand assets from other Canva projects carry directly into a new deck
- Presentation-specific extras — a built-in presenter view, one-click video recording of a deck being presented — are well-integrated rather than bolted on

**Cons**
- Presentations aren't the specialty here the way they are for the other four tools, so AI-assisted deck generation is less deeply tuned for pitch-deck logic specifically
- The 200-credit AI pool on free is lifetime, not monthly, and Pro's usage is generous but still capped, not unlimited
- Advanced brand governance and multi-seat collaboration require stepping up to Canva Business at $20/person/month, same as with any other Canva format

### Beautiful.ai

**What it's for:** people who want the deck to look professionally designed without manually adjusting spacing and alignment on every slide — the smart-layout engine is doing real design work in the background as you type.

**Pros**
- The auto-balancing layout engine is the most hands-off of the group — add a bullet point or a chart, and the slide re-flows itself to stay visually clean
- 300+ smart slide templates are built around this auto-layout logic specifically, rather than being static templates repurposed from a general design tool
- The Team plan's real-time collaborative editing and centralized slide library are well-suited to teams that reuse the same brand slides across many decks

**Cons**
- No ongoing free plan — only a 14-day Pro trial, after which the account needs a paid plan to keep working in it
- The automatic layout engine that's a strength for speed can also fight a user who wants a very specific, non-standard manual layout
- Jumping from Pro (single user) to Team ($50/mo, or $40/mo billed annually) is a large price step if only two or three people need shared access

### Pitch

**What it's for:** teams that present frequently and want a genuinely free, ongoing tier to start on, with a clean and minimal design aesthetic once you're inside the editor.

**Pros**
- The free tier isn't a countdown trial — unlimited presentations and custom templates are available indefinitely, not just for a trial window
- Team-oriented features (shared pitch rooms, team spaces, external guest access) scale up clearly across Plus, Team, and Business without a confusing jump in complexity
- Video upload and PowerPoint export on the Plus tier cover two common gaps in AI-deck tools that treat the web-hosted link as the only output

**Cons**
- Pricing is published in euros, which adds a currency-conversion step for anyone budgeting in dollars
- The 100 AI credits on the free tier are a one-time pool, not a monthly refresh, so ongoing AI-assisted generation requires upgrading
- The design aesthetic is intentionally minimal and opinionated — a strength for a consistent brand look, but less flexible than Canva's template variety for teams that want more visual range

### Gamma

**What it's for:** people whose output isn't only slide decks — Gamma generates documents and simple webpages from the same AI prompt, which is useful if a project needs a one-pager or a landing page alongside the deck.

**Pros**
- One tool covers three output formats (deck, document, simple webpage) from the same AI-generation flow, which the other four tools here don't offer
- The annual per-seat pricing ($9/mo Plus, $18/mo Pro, both billed yearly) undercuts several competitors' entry tiers at the equivalent AI-credit allowance
- Team and Business tiers include per-viewer analytics on published content, useful for tracking whether a shared deck or document actually got read

**Cons**
- Team and Business plans are annual-only with seat minimums (2 seats for Team, 10 for Business), which doesn't fit a very small team well
- Monthly (non-annual) individual pricing is reported inconsistently across sources at the time of writing — confirm the live number on Gamma's own pricing page before committing to month-to-month billing
- The three-format flexibility (deck/doc/webpage) is a genuine strength, but it also means the deck-specific polish is a bit less deep than a presentation-only tool like Beautiful.ai or Pitch

## Which one fits your situation

**You deliver the pitch yourself, live, and want to rehearse it:** Decktopus, specifically for the Loop practice feature — none of the other four tools here have a built-in rehearsal mode, and the 750 monthly AI credits on the entry Pro tier are generous for regular use.

**Presentations are one of several design formats you produce regularly:** Canva. Sharing one Brand Kit and one subscription across social graphics, one-pagers, and decks is worth more than a presentation-specific tool's marginal edge, unless deck output is the primary job.

**You want the least manual layout fiddling possible:** Beautiful.ai. The auto-balancing engine does real work reducing the "make this look less templated" step that eats time in every other tool here — just budget for the fact that there's no ongoing free tier to test it first.

**You want to start completely free and stay there until the team genuinely needs more:** Pitch. The free tier isn't time-limited, which makes it the lowest-risk starting point of the five if budget is the main constraint right now.

**You need decks, one-pagers, and simple webpages from the same input, and don't want three separate tools:** Gamma. The three-format flexibility is unique in this group, though it's worth confirming current monthly pricing directly given the inconsistency across third-party sources.

**Not sure yet, and want to compare the AI-generation step directly:** Start with Pitch's ongoing free tier and Canva's free plan in the same sitting, since both let you build a real deck without a trial clock running. Between the two you'll get a feel for a presentation-specific minimal editor versus a general-purpose design tool, which is the bigger fork in the road than any single feature comparison.

## Common questions

**Does the AI actually save time, or does fixing its output take just as long?**
For a first draft and basic structure, all five tools save real time — turning a rough outline or a paragraph of notes into a slide-by-slide structure in under a minute is consistently faster than starting from a blank canvas. Where the time savings shrink is on polish: expect to spend real editing time on any AI-generated deck before it's ready for an actual audience, regardless of which tool produced it.

**Can I export any of these to PowerPoint if my audience doesn't use the web-hosted version?**
Decktopus, Canva, and Pitch all support PPT or PowerPoint-compatible export on their paid tiers. Beautiful.ai and Gamma both support export as well, though formatting fidelity when converting a web-native, auto-laid-out deck into a static PowerPoint file can shift some spacing and animation details — it's worth exporting and checking a real deck before relying on it for a client meeting.

**Is it worth paying for the AI credits, or should I just build the deck manually?**
That mostly depends on volume. If decks are an occasional need — a handful a month — a free tier (Pitch, Canva, or Gamma's one-time pool) is usually enough. Once deck creation becomes a weekly or more frequent task, running out of AI credits mid-month on a free or entry tier gets old fast, and that's the point where the paid tiers actually earn their price.

**Which of these is best for a solo freelancer versus a team?**
Canva and Pitch both scale down well to a single user without forcing a team-priced plan. Beautiful.ai and Gamma's team tiers get meaningfully more valuable once more than one person needs shared brand assets or a slide library, but their entry single-user tiers are reasonably priced too. Decktopus is priced per single subscription on Pro, with Business as the team step — worth a sales conversation before assuming what that tier costs.

## Where to start

None of these five require a real financial commitment to try, since Canva, Pitch, and Gamma all have a genuinely free way in — the practical move is to build one real upcoming deck, not a test one, in two of them back to back. That's a better signal than any feature list, since the moment where an AI-generated layout gets something wrong is exactly where you'll feel the difference between a tool that lets you fix it in ten seconds and one that fights you.

If the deck needs to be delivered live and rehearsed beforehand, Decktopus's Loop feature is worth trying specifically for that reason. Start free with whichever of the no-trial-clock options fits the immediate need, build a real, upcoming deck in it, and only move to a paid plan once a specific free-tier limit — the AI credit pool, a locked export format, missing brand controls — actually gets in the way of finishing the work.
