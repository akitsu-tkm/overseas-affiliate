## The problem: every email tool claims to be "for creators," and they're not all built the same way

Picking an email marketing platform used to be a fairly narrow decision: you needed to send newsletters and maybe a welcome sequence, and most of the well-known tools did that reasonably well. That's no longer true. The five platforms below now cover genuinely different jobs — some are built around selling a paid newsletter or a digital product, some are built around funnels and automation for an existing business, and some are built as a general-purpose marketing suite that happens to include email. Picking one because it's the name you've heard most often, rather than the one built for what you're actually trying to do, is the most common reason people end up migrating their list a year later.

That migration is also more painful in this category than in most software categories, because your list, your automations, and your sender reputation all move with you — or don't. A workflow that lives entirely inside one platform's automation builder has to be rebuilt from scratch somewhere else, and a sender domain that's built up a good reputation over years doesn't transfer cleanly to a new provider. That's part of why this comparison spends as much time on how each platform is structured — automation depth, monetization tools, how pricing scales as your list grows — as it does on the sticker price on the homepage.

## Three things that actually matter when you're choosing

**1. What the platform is actually optimized for.** Kit and Beehiiv are both built primarily around independent creators and newsletter writers — Kit around automation and selling digital products to an audience, Beehiiv around growing and monetizing a newsletter specifically, including through its own ad network. Mailchimp is a broader marketing suite that happens to include strong email tools, built for small businesses that also want landing pages, basic CRM features, and ad integrations in one place. AWeber and MailerLite sit in between — both are general-purpose email tools, with AWeber leaning toward simplicity and long-tenured small-business users, and MailerLite leaning toward being the budget-friendly option with a surprisingly deep feature set for the price.

**2. How the price actually scales as your list grows.** Every platform here uses some version of contact-based or subscriber-based pricing, but the starting tiers, what counts as a "contact," and how steeply the price climbs are all different. A tool that looks cheap at a few hundred subscribers can become one of the pricier options at five figures, and vice versa — so the number on the homepage matters much less than the shape of the pricing curve for the list size you're planning to have in a year, not the one you have today.

**3. What happens to unsubscribes, and whether monetization tools cost extra.** Some platforms count unsubscribed or unengaged contacts against your plan limit until you manually clean your list; others don't. And if part of the plan is to monetize the list directly — a paid newsletter, a digital product, a sponsorship — it matters a lot whether the platform takes a cut of that revenue on top of the subscription price, or charges nothing beyond standard payment-processor fees.

## Which one fits your business

**Solo creator planning to sell digital products or a paid newsletter:** Kit or Beehiiv are the two built specifically for this. Kit leans toward automation-heavy audience nurturing and one-off digital product sales; Beehiiv leans toward the newsletter itself as the monetized product, with sponsorships as a built-in revenue stream on top of subscriptions.

**Small business that wants email, landing pages, and light CRM in one place:** Mailchimp's breadth beyond pure email is the strongest reason to choose it over the other four, especially if you're not planning to add a separate landing-page or CRM tool.

**Tight budget, need real automation without paying for extras:** MailerLite's Comfort plan is the cheapest full-featured option in this group, and its unlimited sends on paid tiers avoid the surprise costs that come with contact-based send caps elsewhere.

**Long-established small business that values support responsiveness over the newest features:** AWeber's live chat and phone support on both paid tiers, plus its long operating history, make it a reasonable choice for a business that wants a dependable tool more than a cutting-edge one — as long as the lack of a permanent free tier isn't a dealbreaker.

**Team already running a business, adding email as a new channel rather than a core product:** Mailchimp or MailerLite fit more naturally than the two creator-first platforms, since neither Kit nor Beehiiv is built with a non-newsletter, non-creator business as the primary use case.

One workflow note worth sharing: the biggest practical difference between these platforms rarely shows up in a features list — it shows up in how automations feel to build. A tagging-and-condition-based automation that takes twenty minutes to set up in Kit's visual builder can take considerably longer in a platform that treats automation as a secondary feature bolted onto email sending, and that time difference compounds every time you need to adjust a sequence later. If automation is central to how you plan to use the list — not just occasional broadcasts — it's worth spending fifteen minutes inside each platform's free trial specifically testing that workflow before committing.

## Common questions

**Does list size determine which of these is cheapest?**
Yes, and the ranking changes depending on where you are. At a few hundred subscribers, MailerLite and Mailchimp's Essentials tier are typically the least expensive paid options. As a list grows past a few thousand subscribers, the gap narrows or reverses depending on which platform's scaling curve is steeper — which is exactly why it's worth checking each platform's pricing at the list size you expect to have in a year, not just where you're starting.

**Can I switch platforms later without losing my list?**
Generally yes — subscriber emails and basic tags typically export and import cleanly across these platforms. What doesn't transfer cleanly is automation logic (each platform's automation builder works differently) and, to some degree, sender reputation, since a domain's deliverability history is tied to how it's been used, not just to the address itself.

**Do any of these charge extra for selling digital products or paid subscriptions?**
Kit charges a flat 0.6% fee plus standard card processing on digital product sales. Beehiiv charges 0% platform take rate on paid-subscription revenue, with standard payment-processor fees still applying. Mailchimp, MailerLite, and AWeber are not built around direct monetization in the same way and don't have equivalent built-in commerce features.

**Is Kit's recent price increase a sign prices will keep climbing?**
There's no way to know that in advance, but the September 2025 increase was explicitly described as Kit's first in twelve years, which suggests it isn't a platform with a pattern of frequent price hikes — though it's still worth checking the live pricing page rather than assuming any number quoted elsewhere, including in this comparison, will stay accurate indefinitely.

**Which of these has the best deliverability?**
Deliverability depends heavily on your own sending practices — list hygiene, engagement rates, authentication setup — more than on the platform alone, and none of the five publishes directly comparable deliverability benchmarks. AWeber and Mailchimp's longer operating histories give them more mature infrastructure and reputation management tooling, but that's not the same as a guarantee of better inbox placement for any individual sender.

## Where to start

If monetizing an audience directly — through a paid newsletter, sponsorships, or digital products — is the actual goal, Kit and Beehiiv are the two worth testing first, since they're the only ones built around that specific job. Start free with Kit at up to 1,000 subscribers or Beehiiv at up to 2,500, and use the free tier to actually build one automation or one newsletter issue before deciding, rather than judging on the pricing page alone.

If the goal is simpler — a reliable way to send newsletters and light automation as one piece of a broader small business — MailerLite's low entry price or Mailchimp's broader toolset are the more natural starting points, and both offer either a free tier or a no-card-required trial to test the actual day-to-day experience before paying anything.

Whichever platform looks like the right fit, the same rule applies across all five: start free, build out one real automation or campaign before committing, and only then compare the paid tiers side by side — because the tool that looks cheapest on the homepage isn't always the one that stays cheapest once your list, and your automations, actually grow.
