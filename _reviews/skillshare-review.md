## The problem: "learn anything" doesn't tell you what you're actually buying

Every platform in this category uses some version of the same pitch — one subscription, unlimited courses, learn on your schedule. That pitch is technically true for all five tools below, and it's also not that useful for deciding which one to pay for, because "unlimited learning" means something different depending on whether you're trying to pick up a practical skill for a side project, earn a credential a hiring manager will recognize, or just wanted something better than another evening of scrolling.

I ended up comparing all five after a fairly ordinary trigger: wanting to learn a specific practical skill (illustration, in my case) without signing up for a semester-length course. What I found is that these platforms aren't really substitutes for each other so much as they're built around different definitions of "worth it." A platform that's excellent for career-credential building can be a mediocre fit for hands-on creative skills, and vice versa — and the marketing pages rarely make that distinction clear.

That's partly by design. All five platforms are competing for the same subscription-budget line item — "learning" — so the marketing language converges even when the actual product doesn't. A Coursera Plus ad and a Skillshare ad both promise to help you "grow," but one is measured in completed university-affiliated coursework and the other in a finished portfolio piece. Neither claim is false; they're just answering different questions.

## Three things that actually matter when you're choosing

**1. What you're actually building — a skill, a credential, or neither.** This is the single biggest divider in this category. Coursera and LinkedIn Learning are built around content that maps to recognizable outcomes: university-affiliated certificates, professional certifications from companies like Google and IBM, and skills that display on a LinkedIn profile. Skillshare and Udemy are built around practical, often project-based skill-building with no formal credentialing attached. MasterClass sits apart from both — it's explicitly not about certification or systematic skill-building, but about learning from a recognizable expert in a polished, documentary-style format. Picking based on price alone, without this distinction, is how people end up disappointed six months in.

**2. Whether the pricing model is subscription-only or a hybrid.** Four of the five platforms here are pure "pay one price, access everything" subscriptions. Udemy is the outlier — it still sells individual courses a la carte alongside its subscription plan, which matters if you only want one or two specific courses rather than ongoing access to a catalog.

**3. The gap between monthly and annual pricing.** This category has some of the widest monthly-vs-annual pricing spreads in software — multiple platforms here charge two to three times more for month-to-month billing than for the annual-equivalent rate. If you're trying a platform to see whether you'll actually use it, that gap is worth knowing about before you pick a billing cycle.

It's worth adding a fourth, softer factor: how much the format itself matches how you actually learn. Some people finish more when there's a structured syllabus and a deadline-shaped nudge (Coursera's model); others disengage the moment a course feels like homework and do better with a short, self-contained project (Skillshare's model). No amount of catalog size or price comparison fixes a format mismatch — it's worth being honest with yourself about which camp you're in before picking based on the criteria above.

### What "free" actually gets you

None of these five have a full-catalog free tier, but the free-adjacent options differ:

| Tool | Free-adjacent option | What it actually gives you |
|---|---|---|
| Skillshare | Trial only (typically 7 days) | Full catalog access during the trial, then nothing without paying |
| Udemy | No subscription trial, but individual courses can be purchased one at a time | Lets you buy exactly one course without a recurring subscription |
| Coursera | "Audit" mode on many individual courses | Free access to course videos and readings — no certificate, no graded assignments, no instructor contact, and some professional-certificate courses have removed audit access entirely |
| LinkedIn Learning | Trial only (typically 1 month) | Full catalog access during the trial |
| MasterClass | Trial policy varies and is the least consistent of the group | No standing free tier; annual billing only, no month-to-month option at all |

Coursera's audit mode is the most genuinely useful "free" option here, since it's not time-limited the way trials are — it just permanently withholds the credential and interactive parts of the course.

## How they compare on price

| Tool | Entry price | Billing model | What it's really for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Skillshare** | $13.99/mo billed annually (~$168/year); month-to-month runs closer to $32/mo on the web | Subscription only | Practical, project-based creative and business skills |
| **Udemy** | $13/mo billed annually (~$156/year), or $32-35/mo month-to-month — individual courses can also be bought outright | Subscription + a la carte | Broad topic coverage, especially technical/professional skills, with the option to buy just one course |
| **Coursera Plus** | $399/year (~$33/mo), or $59/mo month-to-month | Subscription, with free audit mode on many individual courses | University-affiliated courses and recognized professional certificates |
| **LinkedIn Learning** | $239.88/year (~$19.99/mo), or $39.99/mo month-to-month | Subscription only | Business, software, and career skills tied to your LinkedIn profile |
| **MasterClass** | $120/year (Standard, 1 device), $180/year (Plus, 2 devices + downloads), $240/year (Premium, 6 devices + downloads) | Annual only, no monthly option | Learning from a named expert in a polished, video-first format |

Every platform here runs frequent promotional discounts — Coursera Plus in particular is regularly advertised well below its $399 list price — so the figures above are list prices, not necessarily what you'll be offered at checkout.

## Which one fits your situation

**Want to build a practical, hands-on skill for a side project or freelance work:** Skillshare. The project-based format and low annual price make it the easiest entry point for learning by doing.

**Want one specific course without committing to a subscription:** Udemy's a la carte option. It's the only platform here that doesn't require ongoing payment to keep access to content you've already learned.

**Want a credential that means something on a resume or LinkedIn profile:** Coursera Plus for university and major-partner professional certificates, or LinkedIn Learning if visible, profile-linked skill badges matter more to your career path than a formal certificate.

**Already paying for LinkedIn Premium Career or Business:** Check whether LinkedIn Learning is already bundled in before paying for it separately — it often is.

**Want to learn from a specific well-known expert and enjoy the format as much as the content:** MasterClass, with a clear-eyed expectation that it's closer to premium video content than a structured course.

**Budget-conscious and just want broad access to try a lot of topics:** Skillshare or Udemy's annual plan land at nearly identical prices (~$13/mo) and are the cheapest full-catalog options here by a wide margin over Coursera or LinkedIn Learning.

## Common questions

**Do any of these count as accredited education?**
Only in a limited sense. Coursera hosts real university-affiliated courses and, separately, full degree programs, but Coursera Plus itself is a subscription to course content, not a degree. The professional certificates from partners like Google or IBM carry industry recognition but aren't academic accreditation. Skillshare, Udemy, and MasterClass offer no accreditation of any kind.

**Can I just watch one Skillshare or MasterClass class without subscribing?**
Not really — both are subscription-gated, with only a time-limited trial as the free option. Udemy is the exception in this group: individual courses can be purchased without any subscription at all.

**Is the month-to-month price ever worth it over annual billing?**
Rarely, given how large the gap is on every platform here except MasterClass (which doesn't offer monthly billing at all). Month-to-month makes sense mainly if you know you only need access for a single month and are confident you won't want to continue — otherwise the annual rate pays for itself quickly.

**Which platform has the best video/production quality?**
MasterClass, by a clear margin — it's built around cinematic production values as a core part of the pitch. The other four are closer to standard screen-recorded or studio-lecture formats, with Coursera and LinkedIn Learning generally more polished than the more variable, creator-uploaded catalogs on Skillshare and Udemy.

**Can I cancel and come back later without losing progress?**
For the subscription platforms (Skillshare, Coursera Plus, LinkedIn Learning, MasterClass), canceling generally cuts off access to the catalog, though basic account and progress data is usually preserved if you resubscribe later — the specifics vary by platform, so it's worth checking before assuming progress carries over cleanly. Udemy is the exception: individually purchased courses stay accessible indefinitely regardless of whether you keep a subscription, which is one of the stronger arguments for its a la carte option if you're not sure you'll stay subscribed.

## Where to start

The fastest way to figure out which of these fits is to get specific about the outcome first, not the platform. If the goal is a finished project or a practical skill you want to put to use soon, Skillshare's format is built for exactly that. If the goal is something you can point to on a resume, Coursera or LinkedIn Learning are doing a different job entirely, and it's worth paying their premium only if that credential actually matters for what comes next.

Whichever one looks like the right starting point, the trial (or Coursera's audit mode) is the way to confirm it before committing to annual billing — start free, work through one real class or module, and let that decide whether the subscription earns its keep rather than guessing from a features list.
